Lab 3: A cocinar

  • Due date in D2L
  • Ch. 8
  • Mode Upload
  • Length 1.5–2.5 min

Record a short cooking video in Spanish on your phone. Any dish works: a sandwich, pancakes, ramen. You can prepare stages ahead of time and talk through them.

Your task

Cover all four parts

  • Introducción: what you're making and why you chose it
  • Los ingredientes: show and name each one, where you bought it, and what it cost (preterite: compré, costó, pagué)
  • Los pasos: at least 3 steps in order (primero, después, luego, finalmente)
  • ¡Listo!: show the finished dish and react

Every video

  • Your face is visible and your voice is clear the whole time.
  • Spanish only, in complete sentences.
  • Full, specific answers. One-word or one-sentence answers don't get full credit.
  • The length listed for each lab.
  • One notecard with up to 15 words. Don't read a script.
  • At the start, hold your notecard still in front of the camera for about 5 seconds, close enough to read. Don't just flash it.
  • No translators, AI tools, or outside help.

No face or no audio for most of the video: 0 points, and you can't resubmit. Watch your recording before you submit. The rubric is in the Bongo assignment.

Record in Bongo

  1. Record your video on your phone in a quiet, well-lit spot.
  2. Move the file to your computer (AirDrop, Google Drive, or email it to yourself).
  3. Open the Bongo assignment and choose Upload (not Record). Select your file.
  4. Watch it back: check that your face and voice come through. Then Submit.

Problems? See Troubleshooting.

Troubleshooting

Before you record

  • Use Google Chrome on a laptop or desktop. iPads and phones don't work well.
  • Allow the camera and microphone. If you blocked them, click the camera icon in Chrome's address bar.
  • Close Zoom, Teams, FaceTime, or anything else using your camera.

The recorder won't load

  1. Clear Chrome's cache and cookies, then reload the page.
  2. Try a Chrome Incognito window.
  3. If the D2L home page shows up inside Bongo, open it in Chrome on a computer.
  4. Restart your computer, then try again.

Other things that stop a recording

  • Windows blocks the camera: Settings → Privacy & security → Camera, and turn on Let desktop apps access your camera. Do the same under Microphone.
  • No sound: AirPods and Bluetooth headphones can take over the microphone. Pick your microphone in Bongo's settings, or disconnect them.
  • Don't close the tab until Bongo finishes uploading. Slow Wi-Fi makes this take longer.
  • Plug in your laptop. Low-power mode and sleep can stop a recording.

Uploading a phone video

  1. Move the video from your phone to your computer with AirDrop, Google Drive, or email.
  2. In Bongo, choose Upload (not Record) and select the file.
  3. Use MP4 or MOV. If the file is too large, trim the start or end, or record at a lower resolution.

Still failing? Record outside Bongo

Record one continuous take with your phone or your computer's camera app. Then choose Upload Video in the same Bongo assignment before 5:00 PM. Videos with cuts, joined clips or replaced audio get 0.

Redo your recording

Open the ⋮ menu at the top right of the assignment and choose Reset Submission. You can do this until the due time.

Your responsibility

Making your lab work is your responsibility: your computer, camera, microphone and Bongo, and a video in the required format (face visible, voice clear, and slides shown in slide labs). Complete Lab 0 in the first week, start each lab early, and watch your recording before you submit. A technology problem never extends the deadline, even if you report it before the due time.

  • Only videos submitted in Bongo are graded, not videos sent through D2L, by email, or as a link.
  • The Language Lab doesn't extend a deadline for a technology problem, even one reported before 5:00 PM.
  • If the Language Lab or UNG confirms that Bongo or D2L is down for everyone, or a campus closes, the Lab extends the lab for all sections and posts an announcement.

Still stuck?

Try the Bongo Help Center. Then use the backup for your kind of lab, or come to your campus Language Lab well before the due time. Contacting the Lab doesn't extend the deadline.
